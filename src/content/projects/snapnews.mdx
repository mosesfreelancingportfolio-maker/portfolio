## Overview

SnapNews is a mobile app that summarizes the day's news into short, concise, and relevant stories. Instead of scanning endless headlines, users get clean summaries tailored to their interests — so they stay informed without the information overload.

The project was awarded the **Ignition Grand prize of $6,000** for its clear real-world value and polished execution.

## The problem

People face an overwhelming amount of daily news. Full articles are long, feeds are noisy, and there is no easy way to get just the facts that matter in a glance. Reading becomes a chore and important stories get lost.

## Goals

- Summarize news into short, scannable stories
- Deliver relevant content tuned to each reader's interests
- Make staying informed fast and pleasant on mobile
- Keep the product simple enough for daily use

## My role

Full-stack development: app architecture, news ingestion and summarization flow, API design, and the mobile client.

## Solution

A React Native mobile app paired with a backend that fetches, processes, and summarizes news articles. Concise summaries are served through the API, and the app presents them in an easy-to-scan feed, letting users quickly absorb the news that matters to them.

## Architecture

- **Mobile**: React Native + Expo app for a smooth cross-platform experience
- **Backend**: Python-powered API for fetching and processing news
- **Database**: Firebase for storing user preferences and saved stories

Flow: news is fetched → cleaned and summarized → stored and served to the app → user reads concise summaries.

## Technologies

- React Native / Expo
- Python
- REST API
- Firebase

## Implementation

Designed a news pipeline that ingests articles, condenses them into clear summaries, and ranks stories for relevance. User interests shape the feed, keeping the home screen immediately useful.

## Challenges

- **Summarization quality**: producing genuinely useful summaries, not just extracts
- **Relevance**: matching stories to individual reader interests
- **Data freshness**: keeping the feed up to date without excessive cost

## Lessons learned

The strongest product ideas often come from simplifying an everyday frustration. Focusing on a clean feed of short, relevant summaries kept the app fast, focused, and easy to use.
